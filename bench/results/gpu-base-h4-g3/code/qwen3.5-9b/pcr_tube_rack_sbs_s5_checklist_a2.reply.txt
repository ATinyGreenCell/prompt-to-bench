The issue with `polyhedron` in OpenSCAD is that the faces must be strictly planar and the vertex winding order must be perfect to define an outward normal. A simple pyramid at a corner often fails this check if the vertices are not ordered perfectly counter-clockwise relative to the outward normal for *every* face.

A more robust way to create a 45-degree chamfer on a corner in OpenSCAD without complex polyhedron face ordering is to define the cut plane explicitly using a `polygon` extruded or by subtracting a shape defined by the intersection of the block and a plane. However, since we are in a `difference()`, we can simply subtract a `polyhedron` where the faces are guaranteed to be correct by defining the "slanted" face first or ensuring the base faces are oriented correctly.

Actually, the most reliable method for a corner chamfer in OpenSCAD that avoids manifold errors is to define the pyramid with the slanted face as the "top" and the three orthogonal faces as the "sides", ensuring the winding is consistent. But a simpler, foolproof approach for a corner chamfer is to define the pyramid vertices and faces such that the normals are obvious.
