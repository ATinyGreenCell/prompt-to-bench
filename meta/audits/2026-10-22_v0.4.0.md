# Release audit for v0.4.0: claims, plain language, code and data

**Snapshot.** The v0.4 draft (`9e351fd`, 21 October 2026) and the working tree built on it, 21-22 October 2026.

**Context.** Seb: "lets audit the whole paper, trim the abstract, and check prose in general for clear informative writing style for the biologist unfamiliar with both Ai and CAD. Then edgecase, harden, and release."

Four Claude agents audited the committed draft in parallel, read-only. Each ran in a fresh session, instructed to find faults:
1. every claim new or changed since v0.3.1, recomputed from the records and the repository's scripts;
2. the whole paper's internal consistency, links and references, and the documents that restate it;
3. code, data and reproduction: tests, frozen hashes, regeneration, edge cases and publishing hygiene;
4. a reading as a wet-lab biologist who has never written CAD code and knows AI only as a chatbot.

The lead session applied the fixes below. A fifth agent, started to check every edit, was cut off when that session ended, before it reported. In a new session on 22 October, three fresh agents rechecked all the edits: (A) the paper's numbers and claims, (B) its text, terms, links and numbering, and (C) the other documents and the code. The lead session verified each finding against the records before applying it ("Final checks", below). Like the earlier audits, this was done by Claude, which also wrote the work, so it is not independent review.

  - Table 8's reading and the five prompt variants as lists;
  - two comparisons that were written as prose (v5 and v9 repairs on batch C; v11 against v9) as small tables.

  - "held out" now means only HO-16: Table 8's halves are *in-family* and *out-of-family*, data that paraphrases no task is *clean*, and v11's four families are *left out of its training*;

## Fixed: claims

- **Abstract and §6.7, "every other model passed fewer".** Qwen2.5-Coder 7B and Gemma 4 E4B passed none on either set. Now "every other model that had passed tasks".
- **"None of the gains tuned on the original tasks carried over".** The syntax fixer's gain in files that build did (33 against 9 of 48, p = 7 × 10⁻⁷). Now "only the syntax fixer's gain in files that build carried over", and the fine-tunes are said not to have been retested.
- **Share of the drop.** The abstract said "about half"; §6.7's model gives 59%. Both now say request length "predicts about 60%".
- **Near-misses.** "Every near-miss failed on a real error, not on a strict check" now gives the split: all 37 departed from the request, 30 in a way that would stop the part working.
- **The logistic models of §6.7 were in no committed script, and one result was wrong.**
  - Problem: the paper gave an odds ratio of 0.68 (p = 0.29) after request words and checker items. That fit took some tasks' checker-item counts from attempts scored by an older checker.
  - Cause: `rescore.py` rewrites an attempt only when its verdict or score changes, so a few attempts that passed under both checkers keep the older, smaller item count. Its docstring now says so.
  - Fix:
    - `heldout_unblind.py` takes each task's most common count;
    - its new section 5 fits the models: odds ratios 0.23, 0.52 and 0.75;
    - with errors clustered by task, only the first differs clearly from 1 (p = 0.0008, then 0.19 and 0.73);
    - the paper says the logistic models were added after the planned analyses.
- **HO-16 (§5.6):**
  - "written after these experiments": two round-1 tests were still running when the set was frozen. It now says the set was written by a separate AI session, worded exactly in the final checks below.
  - "25 independently coded correct ones": 16 are recoded, and 9 are variations of a reference.
  - What the method designer saw: also the task ids and categories, per-task counts of checks, mutants and variants, the authoring agent's notes on its weakest checks, and, by mistake, nine mutant file names.
  - Token overlap: given as token Jaccard (at most 0.37), and up to half of a new request's distinct words appear in its closest original.
- **NPU box (§5.3, §6.5, §9, Tables 12-13):**
  - Why four models have two samples. GLM-4.7-Flash and Qwen3-Coder 30B-A3B also passed nothing in two samples but ran a third. The third samples of gpt-oss-20b and Qwen3 30B-A3B Thinking were removed because every attempt hit the token cap, and those of Qwen3 4B and 8B were dropped to save box time (build log, 20 October).
  - The rule for second thinking-on samples compared with the two standard samples then run. gpt-oss-120b's third standard sample, 7, came later.
  - Order and wording:
    - gpt-oss-120b ties Qwen3.6 27B at 5.3 tasks a sample;
    - "room to reason helped three models clearly";
    - Qwen3 30B-A3B Thinking "fell back".
- **Cheaper fixes (§6.6, Table 14):**
  - Five fixes were specified before any ran on the 16 tasks, and two after those results but before they ran.
  - The 16 reference files were used to check that the grammar accepts valid code.
  - The assistant that built the fixes knew the tasks.
  - "Made most files build" held only for compiled features and the live fixer. Templates built 6 of 16, and the offline fixer 92 of 256.
  - The live fixer's baseline is now the three finished standard samples: 15 against 35 of 48, p = 4 × 10⁻⁵; passes 12 against 15, p = 0.33. The pre-registered comparison with the two samples then saved is given in the caption.
  - Smaller points:
    - the grammar "gained nothing and cost time" (2.4 times slower);
    - a planned Bonsai arm was cancelled for futility;
    - Sonnet-written specs are barely more precise (0.38-0.39) but accept 95% of right parts;
    - the 2,880 attempts include the box runs;
    - the offline records' folders are named.
- **New tasks (§6.7):** the run list now names who was not run (gpt-oss-120b and the other store models). The pre-registered order is stated, and the Table 15 caption gives Opus's ceiling settings.
- **Fine-tuning (§6.4):**
  - The 34 training families are "none of them a benchmark task", but several are the same kind of part as one: a funnel, a grid tray, a knob with a D-bore, a lidded box. This is now said.
  - "The first task passed by a fine-tuned model in this study" was not true. A quick validation run of an earlier laptop fine-tune (15 October, `finetune/validation_run/`) passed the PCR-tube adapter, as a re-check with the final checker confirms. The sentence is now limited to the runs reported.
  - "One fewer pass by repair than the untuned base" used one of the base's two standard runs. It now reads "within the base's range (four and one)".
  - Three "next step" sentences describe rounds that were later run, and now say so.
  - v10's score rate is given as 4.8%, not 5%.
  - The better prompt chosen for the fine-tunes was the idioms alone (h1), not h4; the conclusion and the summary say so.
- **Elsewhere:**
  - Opus's ceiling runs on both task sets are described in §5.3.
  - §9's summary of the re-checks is complete.
  - §9's link reference points to §5.3.
  - "the largest code model" is limited to the laptop models.
  - §2 no longer says print-and-fit validation is in progress.
  - The audit count is eight.

## Final checks (22 October)

Three agents rechecked the edited tree, read-only, each in a fresh session: (A) every number and claim in the paper, recomputed from the records and the repository's scripts; (B) the paper's text against the draft, its terms, links, numbering, rendering and style, and this record; (C) the other documents and the code, running each changed script against the draft's version on the committed data and on edge cases. The lead session confirmed each finding against the records before applying it.

**Numbers.** Every number the edits introduced or changed reproduced, including Tables 14 and 15, the logistic models and both new §6.4 tables. Two older numbers were wrong:
- the box run of v5 won its pass by repair on the gel comb's third attempt, not its second (`box-v5-k8`, sample 2);
- v10's mean best scores on the box are 0.51 / 0.39, not 0.52 / 0.39 (`finetune_table.py box-v10`).

**Claims, made exact:**
- **Who wrote HO-16.** "Written by a separate AI session that saw no model results" (abstract, §1, §9, §10) claimed more than the record shows. The agent's brief told it not to open the benchmark's results, and it made no model calls while writing, but it read a few lines of the paper and of the benchmark's README, and after the freeze the same agent ran the Opus ceiling. The text now says the set was written before any model was run on it; §5.6 adds the brief and the ceiling run, and §9 the brief.
- **§6.6.** The last two fixes were specified after four of the five round-1 results, while the grammar test was still running, not "after those results". The funnel's reference file was also used once, to try the request check's measurements.
- **Table 13.** The second thinking-on samples were chosen before the third standard samples ran, except Gemma 4 26B's.
- **Abstract.**
  - Only the seven fixes were pre-registered, not the fine-tuning or the prompts.
  - The fine-tunes trained on their own failures used feedback again but still rarely fixed a part.
  - Gemma's 7-12 tasks are within three attempts (about 3 on the first try). The lab box now says the same.
  - "The better prompt" is now introduced.
  - Near-misses are defined.
- **Other claims:**
  - "Results at a glance" shows selected results, not the best in each setting, and also draws on §6.4;
  - the batch-C runs agree "on both repair measures", not on first attempts;
  - §6.7's logistic model has a baseline for each of the six pooled runs, not for each AI model;
  - the assistant proposed nineteen parts, not twenty;
  - up to 51% of a new request's distinct words and numbers appear in its closest original one;
  - the conclusion says the near-misses were "often", not "mostly", spatial errors;
  - the box's HTTP 502 errors followed the power cycle until the app was logged in again; the earlier wording implied the power cycle was part of the cure.
